Dating Profile Pictures for Men: The 6 Photos You Need

By David, founder of Emerald Dating · October 28, 2026 · 6 min read

Most men lose the match before she reads a word. She sees a dark selfie, a group shot where she can't tell which one is you and a photo with sunglasses, and she swipes left in under a second.

The good news: dating profile pictures for men follow a simple formula. Here is what the apps themselves say works, the six photos every profile needs and how to get them without spending your weekend on it.

Why your photos matter more than anything else

On the big apps, the numbers are not in a man's favor. SwipeStats analyzed more than 7,000 Tinder profiles and 294 million swipes and found that men's average match rate was 5.26%, compared with 44.4% for women. The median man matched on just 2.04% of his right swipes.

When you are fighting odds like that, small improvements compound. Better photos mean more of your likes turn into matches, and more matches mean you spend less time on the app for the same number of dates.

Photos are also the first thing the apps judge. Hinge's own feature called Top Photo, described in its profile refresh guide, continuously tests your pictures and automatically moves the most popular one to the front. In other words, the app is already measuring which of your photos works. Your job is to give it good options.

What Hinge says gets more likes (and fewer)

You don't have to guess. Hinge publishes this on its help page about why you might not be getting matches.

Photos that tend to get more likes:

  • Photos that show your interests
  • Candid photos
  • Black and white photos
  • Sports photos
  • Smiles
  • Photos with friends or pets

Photos that tend to get fewer likes:

  • Selfies, especially bathroom and mirror selfies
  • Posed photos
  • Photos with filters
  • Beach photos
  • Sunglasses

Hinge's profile refresh guide adds four more rules: don't use outdated photos, no photos that hide your face, no more than one group photo and steer clear of gym selfies.

After more than 10 years of helping men with dating, I can tell you this list matches what I see. The men who struggle almost always break two or three of these rules at once.

The 6 dating profile pictures every man needs

Hinge gives you six photo slots, and Tinder lets you add more. Fill six with these, in roughly this order.

1. A clear, solo headshot (your lead photo)

Head and shoulders, facing the camera, natural light, no sunglasses, no hat. This is the photo that answers her first question: what does he look like?

Smile if it feels natural. The old advice that men should never smile came from a single OkCupid analysis, and when Photofeeler replicated it with a matching sample of 7,140 photos, it found no statistically significant difference between smiling and not smiling. The one combination it flagged as harmful was eye contact with no smile. Translation: if you look straight into the camera, smile a little.

This photo also matters for verification. On Hinge, completing Face Check and matching it to a clear solo profile photo earns you a Selfie Verified badge. A lead photo that obviously shows your face makes that easy.

2. A full body shot

She wants to see the whole picture. Standing, relaxed, well fitted clothes, a clean background. A street, a park or a nice hotel lobby all work. A messy bedroom does not.

3. You doing something you actually do

Hinge lists interest photos and sports photos among the ones that get more likes. Running, climbing, cooking, playing guitar, on a boat with a fishing rod. Pick something true. If she matches because of a hobby, it should be a real one you can talk about on the date.

4. One social photo

One photo with friends shows you have a life and people who like you. Hinge's guide says to pass on more than one, so pick your best and make sure you are easy to spot. If she has to guess which one is you, she won't.

5. A candid

A photo where you are laughing, talking or looking away from the camera. Candid photos make the list of what gets more likes because they look like real life instead of a photo shoot.

6. A conversation starter

A dog, a trip, a dish you made, a black and white photo with some character. Something that gives her an easy first message. On Hinge, people can like and comment on a specific photo, so a photo with a story is a free opening line.

What to cut today

Go through your current profile and remove anything on this list:

  • Mirror and bathroom selfies. Hinge names them specifically as the type that gets fewer likes.
  • Sunglasses or hats in more than one photo. Hiding your face is on Hinge's list of things to avoid.
  • Anything older than about two years. Outdated photos are the fastest way to a disappointing first date.
  • Gym selfies. A sports photo of you playing a game is great. A flexing selfie is not.
  • Filters. They read as hiding something.
  • Group photos beyond the first one. Especially ones with women, which raise questions you don't want her asking.
  • Photos with a fish, a tiger or a car you rented for the day. Fine for your friends. Not doing you favors here.

How to get great photos in one afternoon

You don't need a professional shoot, although it helps. You need good light, a decent camera and a friend who will take 200 photos without complaining.

  1. Shoot in natural light. Late afternoon outdoors, or near a big window. Avoid harsh midday sun and overhead indoor lights.
  2. Use the back camera, not the selfie camera. It is sharper, and holding it at arm's length is what makes a photo look like a selfie.
  3. Change outfits and locations. Three outfits and three spots gives you enough variety to fill six slots without it looking like one shoot.
  4. Take far more than you need. Out of 200 photos, you might keep five. That is normal.
  5. Get a second opinion. You are the worst judge of your own photos. Ask a female friend, or use Hinge Friend's Take so someone who knows you can weigh in.

Then let the apps help. Tinder's Photo Selector scans your camera roll on your device and suggests photos based on things like lighting and composition, filtering out group shots. You still choose what goes up. On Hinge, turn on Top Photo and let it test which lead photo performs best.

Pair your photos with a profile that converts

Photos get her attention. Your written profile decides whether she sends a like or a comment. Once your six photos are set, fix your prompts with our guide to the best Hinge prompts for guys, and make sure each prompt shows a different side of you than the photo next to it.

Test one change at a time. Swap your lead photo, give it a week, and see whether your likes go up. Changing everything at once tells you nothing about what worked.
