Bumble Handles: How Men Can Use Them to Get More Dates

By David, founder of Emerald Dating · November 23, 2026 · 7 min read

You meet someone at a friend's party. The conversation is good, the night is ending, and you hit the awkward moment: ask for her number and risk it feeling like too much, or say nothing and lose her forever.

Bumble just gave you a third option. It is called Handles, and if you use it well it turns the people you meet offline into matches you can actually follow up with. Here is how it works and how I would use it.

What Bumble Handles are

On October 14, Bumble announced Handles, a custom username you can share so someone can find your Bumble profile and express interest. Think of it like an Instagram handle, but it only points to your dating profile.

The key details from Bumble's announcement:

  • Only verified members can create one. If you have not verified your profile, that is step one.
  • Search requires the exact handle. Nobody can browse handles or stumble onto yours with a partial search.
  • It is optional. You can opt in or out of being discoverable at any time.
  • Bumble's moderation rules apply to handle names, with extra protections against impersonation and misuse.
  • Availability is limited for now: Handles are live on Bumble on iOS in the US.

Fast Company reported that a handle adds a new way to send a like, and that mutual interest is still required before anyone can message. So a handle does not open a direct line. It gets your profile in front of one specific person, and she still decides whether to like you back.

Bumble's founder and CEO Whitney Wolfe Herd framed it this way in the announcement: "Whether someone catches your eye or a friend wants to introduce you, you can now simply say, 'Find me on Bumble.'"

Why this matters more for men than you think

Most dating advice treats apps and real life as two separate worlds. Handles connect them, and that is good news for any man who is better in person than on a screen.

Here is the problem Handles solve. Plenty of women are not on the apps at all, or are tired of them. Fast Company cited a 2026 Harris Poll that found two thirds of single adults in the US are not using dating apps, and 72% of singles say dating apps feel more transactional than romantic. Those women are still at parties, gyms, weddings and trivia nights. The bottleneck has never been meeting them. It has been the handoff.

Asking for a number is a big ask after a ten minute conversation. Asking for her Instagram tells her you want to scroll her life, and tells her she will see yours. A handle is a smaller ask with a clear meaning: I am interested in you romantically, here is where you can check me out, no pressure.

Smaller asks get more yeses. That is the whole game.

How to pick a good handle

Your handle is going to be said out loud in loud rooms. Pick it for that, not for cleverness.

  1. Make it easy to say and spell. If you have to spell it twice, it is too complicated. Avoid strings of numbers, underscores and letter swaps.
  2. Keep it short. One or two words. She should be able to remember it walking to her car.
  3. Make it a little personal, not your full name. Your first name plus something true about you works well (a city, a hobby, a sport). Your full first and last name gives away more than the feature is designed to share.
  4. Keep it clean. A crude or try hard handle undercuts you before she sees a single photo. Playful is fine. Cringe is not.
  5. Say it out loud before you lock it in. Say "I'm [handle] on Bumble" to a friend. If they wince or ask you to repeat it, change it.

Five ways to use your handle this week

1. At the end of a good conversation

This is the main use case. When the conversation has a natural close, keep it simple:

"I've enjoyed this. I'm not going to make it weird and ask for your number, but I'm on Bumble. Find me at [handle] if you want to keep talking."

Then stop talking about it. No pitch, no follow up questions. The ease is the point.

2. At singles events and group activities

Handles are perfect for the in-person events that are growing fast right now. In my guide to singles events for men, I said the hardest part of an event is turning a conversation into a date before the night ends. A handle gives you a clean exit line with every person you click with, even if you only talked for five minutes.

3. Friend introductions

If a friend says "you should meet my coworker," the old way was a group chat or a forwarded number, which puts pressure on everyone. Now your friend can pass along your handle, and she can look at your profile privately and decide. If it is a no, nobody has to have an awkward conversation.

4. When she is not ready to share her number

Sometimes you ask for a number and she hesitates. Do not push. Offer your handle instead. It respects her caution and keeps the door open. The control stays with her, which is exactly why she is more likely to use it.

5. In places where your dating life belongs

Fast Company noted Bumble imagines people posting their handle on social media. That can work on a personal account where your audience is friends and friends of friends. I would keep it off anything tied to your work, like LinkedIn or a company bio. Your clients and coworkers do not need a link to your dating profile.

What a handle will not fix

A handle is a bridge. What is on the other side of the bridge still decides the outcome.

When she searches your handle, she is going to make a decision in seconds, usually the same night. If your first photo is a blurry group shot or your prompts are generic, you lose the advantage you built in person. She liked you in real life. Your profile now has to confirm that impression, not undercut it.

So before you start handing out your handle, tighten three things:

  • Your first photo: clear, well lit, just you, looking like you did the night she met you. My guide to dating profile pictures for men covers the full set.
  • Your bio and prompts: specific, warm and easy to reply to.
  • Your verification badge: you need it to create a handle anyway, and it reassures someone who only met you once.

Also check your app notifications. She may like you a day or two later. If she does, match, and move to a plan within a few messages, while the memory of meeting you is still fresh.

Things to know before you rely on it

Handles are new, so keep your expectations realistic.

  • It is US and iOS only for now. If you or she are outside that, it may not work yet.
  • She has to be on Bumble. If she is not, a handle is not much use. Read the room and ask for a number or an Instagram instead.
  • It is not a guaranteed match. She can look and pass. That is fine. You lost nothing and saved yourself an awkward phone number exchange.
  • Bumble is changing fast. The company has dropped its women message first rule (I covered how men should write that first message), is testing an AI matchmaker and is being taken private in a deal Fast Company reported at $1.3 billion. Features may shift. Check the app for the latest version of how handles work.

The bottom line

Bumble Handles take the most awkward moment in meeting someone in real life, the number ask, and replace it with a lower pressure invitation. For a busy man that means every party, event and introduction can feed your dating pipeline without extra hours on the apps.
