Bumble Lets Men Message First: How to Write the Opener

By David, founder of Emerald Dating · October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

For twelve years, men on Bumble matched and then waited. That rule is gone. Men can now send the first message, which sounds like pure upside until you notice the catch: you get one shot, and a lot of men will waste it on "hey."

Here is what changed, how the new system works, and how to write an opener that turns a Bumble match into a conversation and then a date.

What changed on Bumble

On August 11, 2026, Bumble announced a new chat experience that ends its famous women message first rule. According to Bumble's own release:

  • Anyone in a match can send the first message. Men included.
  • You get one opening message. You cannot send a second one until she replies.
  • The chat only opens when she replies. Until then, your opener sits there on its own.
  • The reply window is now 72 hours, up from 24.
  • Very short openers trigger a nudge. If you send something like a single word, Bumble prompts you to write something more thoughtful first.

Engadget's coverage adds useful background: same sex matches could always message first, and Bumble's 2024 "Opening Moves" feature already gave men a limited way in by answering a prompt a woman had set. This update makes it the default for everyone.

Why Bumble made the switch

The short answer is that women asked for it. In Bumble's internal data from a survey of 2,267 US women on the app, 66% said they prefer men to send the first message. Bumble also cites commissioned research with 2,000 single US dating app users aged 18 to 35, where nearly two thirds of men and women said they feel more confident when they have a choice in how and when a conversation starts.

Bumble tested the change in Canada in June and July before rolling it out. Per the release, chat initiation went up, and 92% of surveyed women in the test said they still felt in control.

There is a business story here too. Bumble's Q2 2026 results showed revenue down 15.2% year over year and paying users down 16.4%. The app needs more matches turning into conversations, and letting men start them is one of the biggest levers it had left.

Dating coach Cassandra Fleming made a similar point to Inc.: many women are tired of doing all the initiating and are looking for men who show effort on their own.

That last word is the important one. Effort.

What this means for you

For years, a Bumble match was a coin flip on whether she would message at all. Now the ball is in your court, which is good news if you write well and bad news if you do not.

Think about what her inbox looks like now. Every man she matches with can open the conversation, and plenty will send something generic. Bumble even built a nudge into the app to discourage one word openers. Your opener is no longer competing with silence. It is competing with a stack of other messages.

And because you only get one message before she replies, there is no "hey" followed by a better second line. Whatever you send first is the whole pitch.

How to write a Bumble opener that gets a reply

1. Make it about her profile, not about you

The fastest way to stand out is to prove you actually looked. Pick one specific detail from her photos or prompts and build your message around it. A photo at a trailhead, a prompt about her favorite taco spot, a dog in the third picture. Specific beats clever every time.

2. End with an easy question

Your opener should be simple to answer. A question she can reply to in one thumb's worth of typing gets more replies than a paragraph that asks her to explain her life philosophy. Give her something fun and low effort to react to.

3. Keep it to one or two sentences

You have one message, so it is tempting to cram everything into it. Resist. A short, specific opener feels confident. A long one feels like a cover letter.

4. Skip the compliments on her looks

"You're gorgeous" is not an opener. She has heard it, it gives her nothing to respond to, and it reads exactly like every other man in her inbox. Compliment a choice she made (her taste in restaurants, the trip she took) instead of something she was born with.

5. Send it while the match is fresh

The 72 hour window gives her more time to reply, but it does not mean you should sit on the match. Send your opener the same day you match, while you are still top of mind and while she is likely still active in the app.

Examples (written for illustration)

Weak:

"Hey, how's your week going?"

"You're beautiful. What are you up to tonight?"

Strong:

"Okay, I have to ask about the photo on the boat. Was that a planned fishing trip or did someone get talked into it?"

"Your prompt says you're on a mission to find the best espresso in the city. What's the current leader? I need to know if mine is in the running."

"Fellow half marathon person. Are you training for something this fall or just running for fun?"

Notice the pattern. Each one points to something on her profile, shows a little personality and ends with a question she can answer quickly.

Make your profile do half the work

Here is the part most men miss: either person can message first now, so a strong profile can still earn you openers. Make it easy for her to start the conversation if she wants to, and easy for your own opener to land when you do.

Give her something to reply to:

  • Prompts with hooks. A prompt that mentions a specific opinion ("the best pizza in Chicago is not deep dish") invites a response far more than "I love to travel."
  • Photos that tell a story. A photo of you cooking, playing a sport or on a recognizable trip gives her a ready made opener.
  • A clear first photo. Face visible, no sunglasses, no group shot. If she cannot tell which one is you, she will not stop long enough to write anything.

From reply to date

The opener only matters if it leads somewhere. Once she replies and the chat opens, aim to move toward a date within a handful of messages. Long, aimless text threads are where good matches go to die, and a big reason so many men are burning out on the apps, as we covered in why men are quitting dating apps.

A simple, confident ask works best: suggest a specific place and two possible days. "I know a great wine bar near you. Are you free Thursday or Saturday?" is easier to say yes to than "we should hang out sometime."
