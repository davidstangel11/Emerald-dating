Every man on Hinge eventually hits the same screen: you are out of likes for the day, and the app offers to fix that for a fee. So you wonder whether paying would finally get you more dates, or just a lighter wallet.

The question got more interesting this week. Hinge's parent company said it is testing a cheaper plan, and the reason it gave says a lot about who Hinge's pricing is really built for. Here is what you actually get when you pay, what it costs and a simple way to decide whether it is worth it for you.

## What changed this week

On its third quarter earnings call on November 2, Match Group said [Hinge is testing a cheaper subscription tier](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/hinge-tests-a-cheaper-plan-as-users-become-more-fed-up-paying-for-dating-apps/) with extra filters and custom location boundaries. The CFO, Steve Bailey, said it is "designed for women in particular, but also for price-sensitive users." No price or launch date was shared.

The same call explains why. Bailey called Hinge "one of the highest-priced products we have." Its revenue per paying user is almost twice Tinder's, while the share of users who pay is only slightly more than half of Tinder's. In plain terms: fewer people pay on Hinge, and the ones who do pay a lot.

Hinge is also growing fast. Monthly active users were up 12% year over year, and paying users rose 23% to 2.3 million, according to the [TechCrunch report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/hinge-tests-a-cheaper-plan-as-users-become-more-fed-up-paying-for-dating-apps/). More people on the app means more competition for the same women, which is exactly why so many men start wondering whether paying would give them an edge.

## What Hinge+ and HingeX actually cost

Here are the current prices [reported by Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91635059/hinge-is-making-a-surprising-change-to-its-subscriptions-heres-who-stands-to-benefit):

- **Free:** limited daily likes and limited filters.
- **Hinge+:** $89.99 for three months.
- **HingeX:** $99.99 for three months.

For comparison, the same article puts Tinder Plus at about $25 a month and Tinder Gold at about $35 a month. Prices vary by age, city and how long you sign up for, so check what the app shows you before you decide.

## What you get with each tier

This is what [Hinge's own help page](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/38014282744595-Subscription-and-Purchase-Benefits) says each level includes.

### Free

- A limited number of likes per day.
- You see one incoming like at a time.
- One free Rose every week (it does not carry over).

### Hinge+

- Unlimited likes.
- See everyone who liked you at once.
- Sort incoming likes by Last Active and Nearby.
- Advanced filters, including height, children, family plans, education, politics, drinking, smoking and drug use.

### HingeX

Everything in Hinge+, plus:

- **Skip the Line:** your profile is pushed toward the top of other people's feeds for as long as you subscribe.
- **Priority Likes:** likes you send stay at the top of her Likes You page for seven days.
- **See Compatible People Sooner:** Hinge moves profiles you are likely to be interested in to the top of your feed.

On top of the subscriptions, you can buy Roses in packs, a one hour Boost or a 24 hour SuperBoost.

## The honest answer: paying amplifies your profile, it does not fix it

Here is the rule I give every man who asks me this question. **A subscription multiplies what your profile already does.** If your profile turns likes into matches and matches into dates, paying gets you more of both. If it does not, paying gets you more of nothing, faster.

Think about what Hinge+ really sells. Unlimited likes only help if the women you like are liking you back. If you send 8 likes a day and get almost no matches, sending 50 will mostly give you 50 unanswered likes. Visibility from HingeX works the same way: more women see your profile, and if the profile is weak, more women pass on it.

So before you pay, look at your numbers from the last two weeks:

- **Matches per 10 likes sent.** If you are getting at least one or two, your profile is working and more volume will help.
- **Conversations that turn into dates.** If you match but nothing gets booked, the problem is your messages, not your reach.
- **Incoming likes.** If you get a steady trickle on the free plan, your photos and prompts are pulling their weight.

If those numbers are near zero, spend the money on fixing the profile first. ## When Hinge+ is worth it for men

Paying for Hinge+ makes sense when:

- **You run out of free likes most days** and you are already getting matches from the ones you send.
- **You know exactly what you want** and the advanced filters save you time. If you want kids, for example, filtering on family plans cuts out a lot of conversations that were never going anywhere.
- **You have a busy month coming up** and want to see everyone who liked you so you can reply to the strongest options first instead of scrolling one at a time.

For a busy man, the most underrated Hinge+ feature is seeing all your incoming likes at once. Those are women who already showed interest. Replying to them first is the fastest path from the app to an actual date.

## When HingeX is worth it

HingeX is only $10 more over three months than Hinge+, which makes it an easy upsell. It is worth it if:

- Your profile is already strong and you want more women to see it, especially in a competitive city.
- You are fine sending fewer, more thoughtful likes, since Priority Likes keep each one at the top of her page for a week.

It is not worth it if your match rate is low. Skip the Line puts your profile in front of more women, and a profile that is not converting will simply be skipped by more of them.

## When to skip paying entirely

Keep your money if:

- You have not updated your photos or prompts in the last six months.
- You rarely use all your free likes.
- You get matches but the chats die before a date. That is a messaging problem, and no subscription fixes it.

In those cases, use the free plan, make every like count by commenting on a specific photo or prompt, and fix the weak spot first.

## How to get the most out of paying

If you do subscribe, treat it like a short sprint, not an open-ended bill:

1. **Fix your profile first,** then subscribe. You want the extra visibility pointed at your best version.
2. **Buy three months, not a year,** and put a reminder in your calendar a week before it renews.
3. **Reply to incoming likes first** every time you open the app. They already said yes once.
4. **Use filters for deal breakers, not wish lists.** Filtering out everyone under a certain height or with a certain degree shrinks your pool fast and rarely improves your dates.
5. **Comment, do not just like.** Unlimited likes tempt you to send dozens of blank likes. In my experience, a short comment on something specific still starts more conversations.
6. **Track your numbers weekly.** If matches and dates are not up after a month, the issue is the profile, not the plan.
