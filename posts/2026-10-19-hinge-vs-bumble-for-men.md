If you only have 20 minutes a day for dating apps, you cannot afford to spread them across every app on your phone. The two most common shortlists for men who want real dates are Hinge and Bumble, and last week the gap between them got a lot more interesting.

Here is what changed, how the two apps compare for men right now, and how I would split your time.

## What just happened at Bumble

On October 13, Bumble agreed to be acquired by Ithaca Holdings for $1.3 billion, or $6.05 per share in cash, according to [Dexerto's report on the deal](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/dating-app-bumble-sold-for-1-3-billion-after-massive-drop-in-paying-users-3418605/). The deal takes Bumble private a little over five years after it went public at a valuation of roughly $14 billion. Founder Whitney Wolfe Herd will step down as CEO once the deal closes, and Ithaca co founders Ravi Gupta and John Hegeman will take over as co CEOs.

The same report, citing Reuters, puts Bumble's paying users at 3.2 million in the second quarter of 2026, down 16.4%.

That matters to you for one simple reason. A shrinking app means fewer active people to match with, and an app in the middle of a sale and a rebuild is going to keep changing under your feet.

## What is happening at Hinge

Hinge is moving in the opposite direction. In Match Group's second quarter results, [summarized here by GuruFocus on Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/match-group-inc-mtch-q2-050208463.html):

- **Hinge revenue grew 22%** to $204 million.
- **Hinge paying users grew 17%** to 2 million.
- **Hinge monthly active users rose 13%** year over year.
- Hinge is also **testing a lower priced subscription tier.**

Growth is not the same as quality, but for a man trying to get dates, a growing active user base is the best starting point you can ask for.

## Hinge vs Bumble for men: the real differences

### Who sends the first message

This used to be the biggest difference between the two apps. On Bumble, men had to wait for women to message first. That rule is gone. Men can now send the opener, but you only get one message until she replies. We broke down the new rules and how to write that opener in [our guide to Bumble letting men message first](https://blog.emerald.dating/bumble-men-message-first/).

On Hinge, you have always been able to start things yourself by liking a specific photo or prompt and adding a comment. That format rewards men who actually read profiles, which is exactly the skill that gets dates.

### How the apps handle effort

Hinge has been building features that reward effort and punish neglect:

- **Your Turn Limits.** If too many matches are waiting on your reply, Hinge makes you respond or end those chats before you can keep matching. When Hinge [launched it globally](https://hinge.co/newsroom/your-turn-limits), it said responsiveness on the app rose 20% in testing.
- **Signals badge.** Launched in June, the [Signals badge](https://hinge.co/newsroom/signals-badge) shows up on profiles of daters who show consistent effort. To qualify, you need a complete profile, Selfie Verification, an account in good standing that is at least seven days old, and thoughtful activity in at least three of five behaviors, such as adding a specific comment with your like and responding to messages from matches. It reflects your last 30 days and refreshes daily.

For a busy man, this is good news. Hinge is now visibly rewarding the habits that already work: fewer, better likes and fast replies.

### Matching and algorithms

Hinge says its updated recommendation algorithm, which uses deep learning to predict mutual compatibility, contributed to a "double digit increase in matches overall," according to its [2025 product recap](https://hinge.co/newsroom/hinge-2025-product-evolution).

Bumble is in the middle of a much bigger bet. It is testing an AI matchmaker called Bee, which learns your preferences through private chats and then suggests matches, according to [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91545621/your-next-bumble-match-may-be-chosen-by-ai-instead-of-your-thumb). Bumble has said it plans to move away from swiping, and Bee was set to roll out in select markets in the fourth quarter. [Dexerto reported](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/bumble-is-relaunching-without-swiping-as-dating-app-tests-new-ai-matchmaker-3365001/) that the relaunch, called Bumble 2.0, will also bring "chapter based profiles" that replace the standard bio with story style sections.

That could turn out well. But right now it is a promise, not a proven product, and the company that made the promise is about to change owners.

### Size and reach

Both apps are smaller than Tinder. In [Pew Research Center's survey of online daters](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/02/02/key-findings-about-online-dating-in-the-u-s/), 46% had used Tinder, 28% had used Bumble and about a fifth had used Hinge. That data is from 2023, and the growth numbers above suggest the balance between Hinge and Bumble has shifted since.

The same Pew survey found that 64% of recent male online daters felt insecure about how few messages they received. That is the real problem most men are trying to solve, and it is why choosing where to spend your time matters so much.

## So which one should you use?

Here is my honest take after more than 10 years of helping men with dating.

### Make Hinge your main app

If you can only run one app well, run Hinge. It is growing, its format lets you start conversations on your own terms, and its newest features reward exactly what busy men should be doing anyway: sending a handful of thoughtful likes a day and replying promptly. Your profile also carries more weight on Hinge, because prompts give you room to show personality instead of relying on photos alone. If you have not set it up yet, a friend can help, which we covered in [our guide to Hinge Friend's Take](https://blog.emerald.dating/hinge-friends-take-tips-for-men/).

### Keep Bumble as a second app, not a project

Do not delete Bumble. There are still millions of paying users on it, and men can now message first, which removes the old frustration of matches that expired in silence. But I would not invest heavy time rebuilding your whole approach around it until Bumble 2.0 actually launches in your city and you can see how it works.

Keep a solid profile live, check it once a day, and send one strong, specific opener to each match. That is it.

### When to flip the order

There are a few cases where Bumble may deserve more of your time:

- **Your Hinge feed is thin.** In a smaller city, or if your filters are tight, you may simply run out of good profiles on Hinge.
- **Bee arrives in your market.** If the AI matchmaker rolls out where you live, give it a fair two week test and compare the quality of matches against Hinge.
- **Your results say so.** The best app is the one where you actually get dates. Track it.

## A simple two week test

If you are unsure, run both and let the numbers decide:

1. **Clean up both profiles first.** Six clear, recent photos and specific prompt answers. Same quality on both apps so the test is fair.
2. **Give each app 10 minutes a day.** Hinge: a few likes with a specific comment on each. Bumble: one good opener to every new match.
3. **Track three numbers per app:** matches, real conversations and dates booked.
4. **After 14 days, compare dates booked.** Matches are nice. Dates are the point. Put your time where the dates came from.
