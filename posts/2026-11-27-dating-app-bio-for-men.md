Most men treat the bio as an afterthought. They type "6'1, love travel and the gym, ask me anything" in 30 seconds, then wonder why good matches never start a conversation.

Your photos get her attention. Your bio decides whether she swipes right and what she says first. Here is a simple formula, 15 examples you can adapt, and the lines to cut before the busiest dating stretch of the year starts in January.

## Why your bio matters more than you think

Women read bios. Bumble says so plainly in its own guide on [how to write the best Bumble bio](https://bumble.com/the-buzz/bumble-bio): people use them to judge whether they are compatible with you. When your photos are good, the bio is the tiebreaker.

The apps also say the same thing about what works. Tinder's [Year in Swipe 2025](https://filecache.mediaroom.com/mr5mr_tinder/181435/US_Year%20In%20Swipe%202025_Press%20Release.pdf) named "Clear-Coding" as a defining trend: young daters saying what they want, out loud, instead of playing it cool. In a [Pedestrian roundup of advice from Tinder, Hinge and Bumble](https://www.pedestrian.tv/sex-dating/dating-profile-edits-for-maximum-matches/), Tinder's spokesperson put it in three words, "vague bios are out", and Hinge shared that profiles with clear dating intentions get 177% more dates than vague ones.

Specific interests matter too. In [Bumble's 2025 dating trends research](https://bumble.com/the-buzz/bumble-dating-trends-2025), a poll of more than 41,000 members aged 18 to 35, nearly 46% said unique and quirky interests are key to attraction.

So the job of your bio is simple: be clear about what you want, and be specific about who you are.

## The problem with most men's bios

After 10 years of rewriting men's profiles, I see the same four mistakes over and over:

- **The list of nouns.** "Travel, food, fitness, dogs." Tinder's own data shows Foodie, Travel and Gym are among the most common interests on US profiles. If those are your whole personality, you look like everyone else.
- **The resume.** Job title, school, height, done. That is what the profile fields are for.
- **The negative filter.** "No drama. Don't waste my time. Swipe left if..." It reads as bitter, even when you are not.
- **The blank.** An empty bio tells her you did not try, and gives her nothing to message.

The good news: a busy man can fix all four in 15 minutes.

## The four line bio formula

A strong dating app bio for men usually has four parts. You do not need all four every time, but this is the structure I start with for almost every client.

1. **A specific detail about your life.** Not "I like cooking" but "I make a very serious Sunday ragu."
2. **One thing that shows character.** Something you value, a small flex you can back up, or an honest quirk.
3. **What you are looking for.** One clear line. This is the Clear-Coding piece.
4. **An easy hook.** A question, a challenge or a plan she can respond to in one message.

Keep it to 3 to 5 short lines. Write it like you talk. If you would not say it out loud on a first date, cut it.

## 15 dating app bio examples for men

Steal the structure, not the words. Swap in your real details, because specifics only work if they are true.

### Short and confident (best for Tinder)

1. "Engineer who builds furniture on weekends. Currently losing a long war with a walnut table. Looking for something real. Tell me your best Saturday."
2. "I will find the best taco spot in any city within 24 hours. Ambitious at work, lazy on Sunday mornings. Here for a relationship, not a pen pal."
3. "Runs before work, cooks after. Terrible at karaoke, does it anyway. Let's skip the endless texting and grab a drink."
4. "Recently got into climbing and I am humbly bad at it. Big on family, bigger on breakfast. Looking for my person."
5. "Former college rower, current dog uncle. I plan good dates. Your job is to pick the dessert."

### Playful (best for Bumble)

Bumble's bio guide suggests being specific and then "spicing it up" with a joke or exaggeration. These lean into that.

6. "Will drive two hours for good barbecue. Will not drive two minutes for a chain restaurant. Looking for a co pilot with opinions."
7. "Pros: I cook, I call my mom, I remember birthdays. Cons: I will beat you at Mario Kart. Serious about finding something long term."
8. "Ask me about the time I got lost in Lisbon and ended up at a stranger's wedding. Looking for someone curious."
9. "Strong opinions on pizza, weak opinions on almost everything else. Want a relationship with someone who laughs easily."
10. "Building a company by day, perfecting my espresso by night. Still searching for the perfect bagel. Help me look?"

### Grown up and direct (best for Hinge and men over 30)

On Hinge your prompts do most of the talking, but the same rules apply. Hinge told Pedestrian that one word answers and cliches hurt you, and that you should treat the profile like the opening line of a conversation.

11. "I have a career I like, a home I like and a good group of friends. Missing the person to share it with. Ready for a relationship."
12. "Weekdays are busy. Weekends are for farmers markets, long dinners and the occasional bad hike. Looking for a partner, not a project."
13. "Divorced, close with my family and done with games. I am looking for someone kind, curious and a little competitive."
14. "Things I am good at: planning trips, making people laugh, fixing anything with a hinge. Looking for a life partner."
15. "Here for something serious. First date idea: a walk, a coffee and an honest conversation. I will pick the place."

## Lines to delete today

Open your profile and search for these:

- **"Just ask."** She will not. Give her something to work with.
- **"Not on here much."** Then why would she match you?
- **"Looking for my partner in crime."** It is the most worn out line on the apps.
- **Anything starting with "No" or "Don't."** Bumble's guide suggests turning red flags into green flags. Instead of "no picky eaters," write "looking for someone who will try the weird thing on the menu."
- **Your height as the whole bio.** Put it in the field the app gives you.
- **Instagram handles with nothing else.** It looks like you are collecting followers.

## How to tailor your bio to each app

The formula is the same, but each app rewards something slightly different.

**Tinder.** Short wins. Most swiping happens fast, so lead with your best specific line. Tinder's well known Rule of Three (two aligned traits, then a playful twist) is a good shortcut for line one.

**Bumble.** Bumble's guide recommends filling out Interests, Basics and badges first, then using the bio for whatever those fields miss. Since Bumble now lets men send the first message on some matches, a bio with a clear hook also helps her reply to you.

**Hinge.** Your three prompts are your bio. Use one for humor, one for something you care about and one for what you want. Hinge's advice is to mix humor with honest answers.

## Should you use AI to write your bio?

Plenty of people do. In Tinder's Year in Swipe 2025 survey, 76% of young daters said they would use AI in their dating life, with bio prompts among the most popular uses.

AI is fine for a first draft or for brainstorming specifics you forgot about. The risk is that it writes bios that sound like everyone else's AI bio. Use it to get unstuck, then rewrite in your own voice and add details only you would know. ## A 15 minute bio rewrite

If you do nothing else this weekend, do this:

1. **Minutes 1 to 5.** Write down 10 specific things about your life: a dish you cook, a place you love, a hobby you are bad at, a weird skill.
2. **Minutes 5 to 10.** Pick the two most interesting. Add one line about what you want and one hook.
3. **Minutes 10 to 13.** Read it out loud. Cut anything you would not say to her face.
4. **Minutes 13 to 15.** Send it to one honest friend and ask: "Would you want to message this guy?"

Then leave it for a week and watch your match quality and first messages, not just your match count.
