You matched, the chat is going well, and then it just keeps going. A week later you are still trading jokes, nobody has suggested a plan, and the conversation quietly fades out. Sound familiar?

For busy men, this is where most of the wasted time on dating apps lives. Not in swiping, but in conversations that never turn into a date. Here is how to ask a girl out on a dating app at the right moment, with the right words, so more of your matches end up across a table from you.

## Why so many good chats never become dates

You are not imagining it. When Hinge launched its Date Ideas feature, its lead relationship scientist Logan Ury said that ["three-quarters of Hinge daters surveyed have gotten stuck in chats that never turned into dates."](https://hinge.co/newsroom/date-ideas-feature) Three out of four. That is the normal outcome when nobody takes the lead.

The reason is simple. Asking someone out feels risky, so both people wait for a better moment. Meanwhile, she is talking to other matches, life gets busy, and the energy drains out of the chat. The man who asks clearly and early usually beats the man who waits for the perfect opening.

The good news: the bar is low. If you ask well, you are already ahead of most of the men in her inbox.

## When to ask: sooner than you think

The best time to ask is when the conversation has momentum, not after it has peaked. In practice that usually means:

- **After 6 to 10 good back and forth messages,** or one or two days of steady chatting.
- **Right after a high point,** like a shared laugh, a common interest or a story she clearly enjoyed telling.
- **Before the chat turns into pen pals.** If you are past a week of daily messages with no plan, you are late. Ask now anyway.

You do not need to know everything about her before a first date. The point of the date is to find out whether there is chemistry in person, and no amount of texting answers that question.

## How to ask: be specific, not vague

The most common mistake men make is asking in a way that puts all the work on her. "We should hang out sometime" sounds relaxed, but it is not an invitation. She has to decide whether you mean it, what you mean and when. Often the reply is "yeah totally" and nothing happens.

A good ask has three parts:

1. **A hook from your conversation.** Tie the plan to something you already talked about.
2. **A specific plan.** A place or an activity, not "something."
3. **Two time options.** Easy to say yes to, and it shows you are serious.

Here is the difference:

- **Weak:** "Would you want to grab drinks sometime?"
- **Strong:** "You've convinced me I need to try that mezcal place you mentioned. Want to go Thursday or Saturday? I'm free after 7."

The strong version takes ten seconds longer to write and makes it far easier for her to say yes. It also signals what women say they actually want. In a [Hinge survey of its daters](https://hinge.co/newsroom/date-ideas-play), 89% of women agreed it is more impressive when someone takes the time to plan a thoughtful date than when they spend a lot of money. You do not need an expensive dinner. You need a plan that shows you were listening.

## Ready to copy: asks that work

Adjust these to your own voice, but keep the structure:

- **Shared interest:** "Okay, any woman with opinions on ramen this strong deserves a proper test. There's a spot near me I love. Tuesday or Wednesday?"
- **Low key:** "I'm enjoying this, but I think we'd have a better conversation over a drink. Are you around Thursday evening or Sunday afternoon?"
- **Activity:** "You said you've never done trivia night. I know a good one on Wednesdays. Want to be my teammate?"
- **Busy week:** "My week is packed, but I'd rather see you than keep texting. Coffee Saturday morning, or a drink Sunday?"

Notice that none of these ask permission to ask. They just ask. Confidence on a dating app is mostly about being clear.

## Use Hinge Date Ideas to skip the guessing

If you are on Hinge, use the [Date Ideas feature](https://hinge.co/newsroom/date-ideas-feature). It lets you add three preferred first date activities to your profile, chosen from options like drinks, coffee or tea, dinner, a walk, live music, mini golf, trivia night, museum and karaoke, or one you write in yourself. It is optional and separate from your three prompts. Hinge says it is available in English in the US, the UK, Canada and Australia.

It works both ways:

- **On your profile:** pick three activities you would genuinely enjoy and can book easily. That tells women you are on the app to meet, not to collect pen pals.
- **On her profile:** when she has Date Ideas, you can pick one of her three activities and a time window (this week, this weekend, next week or next weekend) before you like or comment.

That second part is a quiet advantage. If you match after choosing "a walk this weekend," the ask is already half done. Your first or second message can simply be: "So, that walk this weekend. Saturday morning by the river?"

Hinge reports that during its test, [54% of daters said the feature made them feel more confident asking someone out](https://hinge.co/newsroom/date-ideas-feature), and that it led to more matches and dates overall. Since it costs nothing to add, there is no reason not to.

## Pick a first date that is easy to say yes to

For a first date from an app, the ideal plan is short, public, close to both of you and easy to extend if it goes well. Think drinks or coffee for about an hour, or a simple activity.

Activity dates are worth considering. In the same [Hinge survey](https://hinge.co/newsroom/date-ideas-play), nearly half (48%) said trying a new activity together is what lets them connect most deeply with a potential partner. Mini golf, a gallery, trivia or a market walk gives you something to talk about and takes the pressure off constant eye contact.

What to avoid on a first date:

- Long dinners with no exit if it is not clicking.
- Anything that needs a big commitment of time or money.
- A plan that is far from her side of town.

## What to do after she says yes

The ask is not finished until the date is on the calendar. Once she agrees:

1. **Lock in the day, time and place in one message.** "Great, Thursday at 7:30 at the bar on Main. Looking forward to it."
2. **Move to text if it feels natural,** but do not make it a condition.
3. **Send a short, friendly confirmation the day of.** "Still on for tonight? I'll grab us a table." This cuts last minute cancellations.
4. **Keep the chat light until then.** Save your best stories for the date.

## What to do if she says no, or does not answer

A "no" is useful information. Reply once, kindly, and move on: "No worries, it was fun chatting. Good luck out there."

If she says "I'm busy this week," that is not always a no. Offer one alternative: "No problem, how does next week look?" If she does not suggest a time herself, take that as your answer.

If she simply goes quiet after your ask, do not chase. One light follow up a few days later is fine. Beyond that, your time is better spent on matches who are excited to meet you. ## A simple system for busy men

If you only remember one thing, make it this: every good conversation should get a clear, specific ask within a couple of days. Treat it like a habit, not a big moment.

- Ask in the first few days of a good chat.
- Use a hook, a specific plan and two time options.
- Add Hinge Date Ideas to your profile and use hers when she has them.
- Confirm the day of the date.
- Let silence be an answer and move on.
