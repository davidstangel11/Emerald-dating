You are tired of swiping, and every article says dating is "moving offline." So you look up singles events in your city and find a speed dating night, a mixer at a hotel bar and a pottery class. Which one is actually worth a weeknight?

Most men pick badly, show up once, stand by the bar and decide events do not work. Here is what the data says about which formats are growing, how to choose one, and how to walk out with a date instead of a drink receipt.

## Why singles events are having a moment

The shift is real, and it is showing up in hard numbers.

Eventbrite's [Modern Meet-Cute Index](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/press/newsroom/the-modern-meet-cute-index/) compared US events on its platform in the first half of 2026 with the first half of 2025. It only counted public, in-person events that explicitly signaled meeting intent, with phrases like "singles," "mixer" or "come solo." Among the findings:

- Experiences using "meet-cute" language grew **117%**.
- Trivia nights were up **403%** in events and **388%** in attendance, led by Chicago, Boston and Raleigh.
- Mahjong events grew **235%**, with attendance up **272%**.
- Birdwatching meetups grew **131%** and pilates socials **84%**.

The same release cites Eventbrite's Social Study: **58%** of young adults want socializing to be a secondary part of an experience, and **69%** want events that combine different interests. In plain English, people want to meet someone while doing something, not stand in a room labeled "singles."

The apps have noticed. In June, Bumble launched [Plans](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/bumble-launches-paid-plans-feature-for-in-person-group-dating/), a paid feature for curated in-person group dating events. You RSVP and pay, you can bring a friend for an extra fee, the location is revealed after payment, and you can match with people you met afterward in the app. Tinder has run its own group features too, which I covered in [Tinder Group Hangouts explained](https://blog.emerald.dating/tinder-group-hangouts/).

Part of the reason is app fatigue. [Fast Company reported](https://www.fastcompany.com/91591332/gen-z-is-over-dating-apps-this-403-surge-reveals-what-theyre-doing-instead) that, according to AppsFlyer, 65% of dating apps downloaded in 2024 were deleted within a month, a figure that later climbed to 69%.

## Are singles events worth it for a busy man?

Yes, with one condition: you treat them as one channel, not a replacement for your apps.

Here is the honest trade. An app lets you meet people from your couch in ten minute bursts. An event costs you an evening, a ticket and the commute. In exchange you get something apps cannot give you: she sees how you talk, how you carry yourself and whether you are fun to be around, all before anyone has to judge a photo.

That favors men who are better in person than on a screen, which is most of the men I work with. If your profile undersells you, a good event is a shortcut past it.

The catch is that a bad event is a wasted night. So the choice matters more than the effort.

## The four main types of singles events

**1. Speed dating.** Short timed conversations, usually a few minutes each, with a structured rotation. Good if you want volume in one evening and you are comfortable opening conversations quickly. Check the age range and how the organizer balances the guest list before you buy.

**2. Classic mixers.** A bar or lounge, a name tag, maybe an icebreaker. These are the format people are moving away from. With no shared activity, everyone clumps with the friend they came with. Skip them unless the host has a strong track record.

**3. Activity events with meeting intent.** Trivia, mahjong, pottery, cooking classes, pilates socials, run clubs. This is where Eventbrite's numbers are growing fastest, and it is where I would start. The activity gives you something to talk about, and nobody has to pretend they came only to flirt.

**4. App hosted events.** Bumble Plans and similar features run by the apps. The upside is that everyone attending is already on the app and expects to connect afterward. The downside is that availability depends on your city, and you are paying up front.

## How to pick an event worth your evening

Before you buy a ticket, check five things:

1. **Explicit singles or "come solo" language.** Eventbrite only counted events that said so, for a reason. A pottery class with no meeting intent is mostly couples and friend groups.
2. **An age range that fits you.** If the listing says nothing, ask the organizer. A great event full of people ten years outside your range is still a wasted night.
3. **Gender balance.** Good organizers manage this and say so. If they cannot tell you, assume it is skewed.
4. **Built in rotation.** Teams that shuffle, tables that change, partners that switch. Rotation does the hard part (meeting new people) for you.
5. **Reviews from past events.** Look for comments about the crowd and the host, not the venue.

Then pick something you actually enjoy or would not mind getting better at. You will be more relaxed, and relaxed reads as confident.

## How to work the room without being that guy

You do not need lines. You need a few simple habits.

- **Arrive early.** The first 20 minutes are the easiest time to talk to anyone, before groups form.
- **Go solo or with one friend who will split up.** A pack of guys is the least approachable thing in any room.
- **Open with the activity.** "Have you played mahjong before, or are we both lost?" works because it is about the room, not about her.
- **Talk to everyone.** Be friendly with the men and the women in the group. People notice who is warm with everyone and who only turns it on for one person.
- **Keep it short the first time.** A few good minutes, then move on. You can come back. Lingering is what makes people feel cornered.
- **Do not drink your nerves away.** One drink is plenty. You want to remember her name.

## Turning a conversation into a date

This is where most men lose it. They have a great chat, say "nice meeting you" and hope to run into her next month.

If you clicked, ask before the night ends. Keep it light and specific: "I had fun talking to you. Want to grab a drink this week? I can text you." Then get her number or connect on the app, and send a short message the next day that references the event and suggests a day. The same hook plus plan formula from [how to ask a girl out on a dating app](https://blog.emerald.dating/how-to-ask-a-girl-out-on-a-dating-app/) works here, and it is easier because you already met.

If she says no or seems unsure, be gracious and keep enjoying the event. Everyone in that room will see how you handle it.

## How to fit events into a busy week

You do not need to go every night. A simple system:

- **One event every two weeks.** Pick it on Sunday, buy the ticket, put it in your calendar like a meeting.
- **Keep your apps running.** Events are a second channel, not a replacement. The men who get the most dates use both.
- **Match the event to your apps.** If you are on Bumble and Plans runs in your city, try one. You will have something in common with everyone there before you arrive.
- **Track what works.** After three events, you will know which formats and organizers produce numbers and dates. Drop the rest.

## The bottom line

Singles events are growing because people want to meet someone while doing something. For a busy man, the best formats are activity events with clear meeting intent, good gender balance and built in rotation. Arrive early, open with the activity, talk to everyone and ask before the night ends. Use events alongside your apps, not instead of them.
