Between Thanksgiving and New Year's, your calendar fills with work parties, travel and family, and dating usually gets pushed to "after the holidays." Then January arrives and you are starting from zero again.

Dating during the holidays is harder to schedule, but it is also when a lot of singles are most open to meeting someone. Here is how I have men handle the eight weeks from late November to early January so they finish the year with real momentum instead of a dead inbox.

## Why the holidays are worth your time

The apps do not go quiet in December. They get busier. Back in 2016, [Tinder said matches rose 23% over Thanksgiving weekend](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/tinder-sees-23-match-increase-over-thanksgiving-period/) compared with a normal weekend, with more than 17 million matches, and some cities like Detroit and Houston jumped by 40% or more. That is an older number, but the pattern of people swiping from their hometown couch is not new.

The surge keeps building into January. Hinge reported [a 31.2% increase in likes and a 24.5% rise in messages](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/dating-sunday-drives-annual-surge-in-app-activity-for-2026/) on Dating Sunday, the first Sunday of the year, compared with an average Sunday.

So the holiday window is not dead time. It is the warm up for the busiest stretch of the year. The men who keep a few conversations alive through December walk into January with dates already lined up.

## The holiday calendar, week by week

This year Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26, Christmas falls on a Friday and New Year's Eve is a Thursday. Plan around that.

### Now through Thanksgiving: get your profile ready

Do your profile work in early November, before the travel starts. Swap in one or two recent photos, ideally with a fall or winter feel, and make sure your prompts give her something to reply to. If your photos need work, our guide to [dating profile pictures for men](https://blog.emerald.dating/dating-profile-pictures-for-men/) walks through the six shots that matter.

Then book one or two dates for the week before Thanksgiving. Weeknights are easier now than they will be in three weeks.

### Thanksgiving week: decide where you are swiping

If you fly home for Thanksgiving, your app will show you people in your hometown. That can be fun, but be honest with yourself. Matching with someone who lives 900 miles from you is a nice chat and rarely a relationship.

Two simple rules:

- **If you are home for a few days,** set your location back to your own city, or keep your distance setting tight and only match with people who also live where you live.
- **If you want to meet someone back home,** say so early: "I'm in town until Sunday, want to grab a coffee Friday?" Clear and low pressure.

The Wednesday night before Thanksgiving is a big night out in a lot of hometowns. It is fine to go. Just do not let a hometown flirtation replace the matches waiting for you in your own city.

### December: work the calendar, not the inbox

December weeknights disappear fast. Here is how to keep dating without it eating your month:

- **Book first dates early in the week.** Monday through Wednesday is usually wide open while Thursday and Friday fill with parties.
- **Keep dates short.** A 60 to 90 minute drink or a walk through a holiday market fits around a crowded schedule and is easy for her to say yes to.
- **Use the season.** Holiday lights, a skating rink, a hot chocolate stand or a festive bar pop up are better than "drinks somewhere." Specific seasonal plans give her an easy reason to say yes.
- **Reply within a day.** People travel, get busy and drop chats. If you keep momentum, you stand out. Our post on [getting ghosted on dating apps](https://blog.emerald.dating/getting-ghosted-on-dating-apps/) has the full playbook for keeping chats alive.

### The week between Christmas and New Year's

This week is underrated. Many people are off work, back in their cities and bored. If you are in town, it is one of the easiest weeks of the year to get a date on the calendar. Send a few messages on December 26 and 27 and suggest a plan for the following days.

### New Year's Eve and the first week of January

Do not make a first date on New Year's Eve. The pressure is too high, everything is overpriced and crowded, and a midnight kiss with a near stranger is a lot of weight for one evening. If you have been seeing someone for a few weeks, inviting her to a party you are already going to is great. If you just matched, suggest January 2 or 3 instead.

Then use the first Sunday of January. With the Dating Sunday surge in mind, freshen your profile the week before and plan to be active that afternoon and evening.

## Holiday parties: do you bring a date?

Most singles go solo, and that is fine. In a [Talker Research survey of 2,000 single Americans](https://talkerresearch.com/what-american-singles-are-planning-for-holiday-hook-up-season/), twice as many said they would attend holiday gatherings alone rather than with a date (44% vs. 21%).

A simple rule for the office party or a friend's party:

- **Seeing her a few weeks?** Inviting her to a friend's party is a nice step. A work party is more pressure, so ask if she even wants to go.
- **Just started?** Skip it. A first or second date surrounded by your coworkers is not a date. It is a performance review.
- **Going solo?** Holiday parties are good places to meet people through friends. Let your friends know you are single.

## Should you bring her home for the holidays?

Usually not yet. In a [Tawkify survey of 1,000 Americans](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/survey-holiday-introductions-can-strain-early-relationships/), 26% said they had brought a partner home for the holidays and later regretted it, and 24% said they had felt pressured to bring someone home before they were ready. The same survey found 72% feel bringing someone home makes a relationship feel "official," and 39% think couples should date at least six months before a holiday family intro.

My advice: if you have been dating less than two or three months, do not make family introductions part of the holidays. Plan your own celebration together before or after you each travel. It takes the pressure off and still shows you care.

## Gifts for someone you just started seeing

This trips men up every year. Keep it proportional:

- **A few dates in:** No gift needed. A small gesture like picking her up a coffee or a holiday treat on your next date is plenty.
- **About a month or more, but not exclusive:** Something small and thoughtful under $50. A book she mentioned, a candle from a shop she likes, tickets for a date you will take together.
- **Exclusive:** A real gift, but match the stage. Something tied to a conversation you had beats something expensive.

An experience you share, like a cooking class or a show in January, works at almost any stage. It is a gift and a second plan in one.

## What not to do this season

- **Do not pause the apps for six weeks.** That is the most common mistake. You lose your conversations and start January cold.
- **Do not text your ex because you are bored at your parents' house.** You know how that ends.
- **Do not let the holidays rush a relationship.** The calendar creates pressure to define things fast. Let it move at its real pace.
- **Do not overbook.** Two good dates a week beats five rushed ones you are too tired to enjoy.
