Most men's Hinge profiles have the same problem: they are written by the one person who is worst at describing them. You either undersell yourself or you oversell yourself, and both read badly. Hinge's Friend's Take feature fixes that by letting people who know you say the things you cannot say about yourself.

Used well, it is one of the easiest profile upgrades on any app right now. Used badly, it is an inside joke that makes a stranger swipe left. Here is how to get it right.

## What Hinge Friend's Take is

Hinge [launched Friend's Take on July 15, 2026](https://hinge.co/newsroom/friends-take). It lets friends, family and other people in your life add short reflections to your profile in text, voice, video or photo form. It is available to Hinge users globally except in India.

The thinking behind it comes from Hinge's own research, published with the launch:

- 79% of daters turn to friends and family for dating advice.
- 71% of daters on Hinge say they want help with their profiles.
- Only 46% actually go and get that help.

That gap is the opportunity. Plenty of daters want a better profile but never ask anyone to look at it. Friend's Take turns "ask a friend" into a built in feature, and the result shows up right on your profile where she can see it.

## How Friend's Take works, step by step

According to [Hinge's help center](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/51737136093203), the setup is quick:

1. Tap your photo icon in the bottom right, then the pencil icon next to your profile photo.
2. Under your profile photo, tap **Friend's Take**, then **Share Link**.
3. Send the link to anyone you want to hear from. They do not need a Hinge account.
4. Your friend picks a prompt about you, answers by text, voice, video or photo, and adds a name or nickname plus how they know you.

A few limits worth knowing:

- Each link works for 72 hours or until it gets 10 submissions, whichever comes first.
- You can have up to 10 active links at once.
- You get a notification for each new take. You can add it, delete it or ignore it.
- You can show up to three takes on your profile and keep up to 50 waiting in your hub ([per Hinge's announcement](https://hinge.co/newsroom/friends-take)).
- You cannot edit a take. You either use it or you do not.
- Once a take is live, other members see the content plus your friend's name and their relationship to you.

That last point matters. Your friend's name and "college roommate" or "sister" show up on your profile, so choose people you are happy to have associated with you in public.

## Why this matters more for men than you think

The hardest thing to show on a dating profile is character. Photos show what you look like. Prompts show how you talk. But anything you write about yourself being loyal, funny or generous is just a claim, and she has probably read that exact claim on plenty of other profiles.

A take from someone else is different. It is a second opinion. When your sister says you are the one who organizes every family trip, that lands as evidence instead of bragging. It answers the quiet question behind every swipe: what is this guy actually like to be around?

For busy professionals in particular, it also softens the "all work" impression. If your photos lean toward suits, conferences and city skylines, one warm take from an old friend about how you show up for people does a lot of work.

## Who to ask (and who not to)

You only get three slots on your profile, so be picky about who you send the link to.

**Good choices:**

- **A sister, female cousin or close female friend.** She knows what women notice and will usually write something specific and warm rather than a roast.
- **A longtime friend who has seen you at your best.** College roommate, groomsman, the friend you have known for 15 years. Long history reads as stable and trustworthy.
- **Someone from a different part of your life.** A teammate from your rec league or a friend from your running club. It shows you have a life outside your job.

**Skip:**

- **Your group chat.** Sending the link to eight guys at once is how you end up with 10 jokes about your fantasy football team.
- **Coworkers you manage.** Awkward for them, and it pulls your profile back toward work.
- **Exes.** Even if you are on great terms, it raises questions you do not want to answer on a first date.
- **Your mom, as your only take.** One family take is great. A profile where the only praise comes from Mom reads younger than you are.

## What a strong take looks like

The best takes share three traits: they are specific, they show behavior instead of adjectives, and they hint at what it is like to date you.

Compare these two examples (written for illustration):

> "Mike is the best. Super funny and a great guy. 10/10."

> "Mike is the friend who remembers everyone's birthday and somehow books the dinner reservation nobody else could get. He also makes a very serious carbonara."

The first one could describe anyone. The second gives her a picture and an easy opener ("okay, I need to know about this carbonara").

You cannot edit what friends send, but you can steer it. When you share the link, add a short note:

> "Hinge lets friends add a few lines to my profile. Could you write something specific, like a story or a habit of mine? Keep it positive and nothing too inside joke. Thank you!"

That one message will raise the quality of what comes back more than anything else you do.

### Use the format options

Text is the default, but voice is worth a look. Back when Hinge [introduced voice prompts in 2021](https://hinge.co/press/voiceprompts), it reported that 65% of Hinge singles believe hearing someone's voice would help them judge interest in a match. A friend's voice saying something kind about you feels personal in a way text does not. Just make sure the audio is clean and short.

A photo take can work too, as long as it is a good photo of you. Remember Hinge's rules: takes should not include other people, and they should not include private or sensitive details like health or financial information. Ask friends to keep it simple.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Using all three slots right away.** Start with the single best take. If you get a second one that shows a different side of you, add it. Three mediocre takes are worse than one great one.

**Roasts.** A little teasing can be charming. A take that jokes you are always late, always broke or "a lot" is a red flag to a stranger, even if your friends find it hilarious.

**Repeating your prompts.** If your prompt already says you love to cook, a take about cooking adds nothing. Use takes to fill gaps: if your profile is all travel and work, let a friend talk about loyalty or humor.

**Never rotating.** Hidden takes are kept for the life of your account and show up under the Not Shown tab, so you can swap them in and out. If your matches have been slow for a few weeks, try a different take before you rebuild your whole profile.

**Treating it as a fix for weak photos.** Friend's Take is a finishing touch. If your photos are blurry, outdated or all sunglasses, no take will save the profile. Fix the basics first.

## How it fits into the rest of your profile

Think of your Hinge profile as three layers:

1. **Photos** get you noticed.
2. **Prompts** start conversations.
3. **Friend's Take** builds trust.

Each layer has a job. Your photos should show your face clearly and a real life around it. Your prompts should give her something easy to reply to. A take should make her feel like she already has a reference before she meets you.
