Most men on Hinge do the hard part and then waste it. They find a woman they actually like, tap the heart, and send it with nothing attached. Or they type "hey" and hope.

That is the single most expensive habit on the app. Here is how to write Hinge opening lines that get noticed, with examples you can adapt in under a minute.

## Why your opening line matters more on Hinge

On Hinge, your first message usually goes out with your like, before you have even matched. She sees it next to your profile when she decides whether to match back. So your opener is not small talk. It is part of your pitch.

Hinge's own numbers make the case. In its [Convo Starters announcement](https://hinge.co/newsroom/convo-starters), Hinge says 72% of daters are more likely to consider someone when a like includes a message, and that likes sent with a comment are twice as likely to lead to a date.

Read that again if you are short on time. A ten second comment roughly doubles the value of every like you send. Nothing else on the app gives you that return for that little effort.

## Comment on prompts more than photos

Hinge lets you like and comment on either a photo or a written prompt. Both work, but prompts give you more to work with.

In its [Prompt Feedback release](https://hinge.co/press/prompt-feedback), Hinge reported that in 2024, likes on text prompts were 47% more likely to lead to a date than likes on photos. That makes sense. A prompt tells you what she thinks, what she does and what she wants. A photo mostly tells you what she looks like, and commenting on looks is the opener she has already seen fifty times.

My rule for clients: comment on a prompt when there is a good one. Use a photo only when it shows something specific she is doing, like a race finish, a trip or a dog with a lot of personality.

## The three part formula for a Hinge opener

The best Hinge opening lines I have seen in 10 years of doing this follow the same shape:

1. **Notice something specific.** Pick one detail from her profile that you actually find interesting.
2. **Add a little of you.** A short opinion, a guess or a related fact about yourself.
3. **End with an easy question.** Something she can answer in one line without thinking hard.

Keep the whole thing to one or two sentences. You are opening a door, not writing a cover letter.

Hinge's Lead Relationship Scientist, Logan Ury, gives the same core advice on the [Hinge newsroom](https://hinge.co/newsroom/convo-starters): skip the generic "hey" and share something specific.

## Hinge opening lines you can adapt

These are templates, not scripts. Swap in her details and your own voice.

### When her prompt mentions food

- Her prompt: "The way to win me over is... finding the best tacos in the city."
- Your line: "Bold claim, because I have a strong taco ranking. Is your number one a sit down spot or a truck?"

### When her prompt is about travel

- Her prompt: "I'm convinced that... Lisbon is the best city in Europe."
- Your line: "Lisbon over Barcelona is a brave position. What sealed it for you, the food or the views?"

### When she gives you a playful challenge

- Her prompt: "I bet you can't... beat me at Mario Kart."
- Your line: "Rainbow Road or a track of my choosing? I need to know what I am agreeing to."

### When her prompt hints at a date

- Her prompt: "Together, we could... find the best cocktail bar in town."
- Your line: "I have two candidates for that title. Want to settle it in person, or should I make my case here first?"

### When you comment on a photo

- Her photo: hiking with a mountain view behind her.
- Your line: "That view looks earned. What was the trail, and was it worth the climb?"

### When her profile is light on detail

Some profiles give you very little. Pick the one thing that is there and be honest about it.

- Your line: "Your dog is clearly running this operation. What is his name and how much trouble is he?"

Notice what all of these do. They show you read her profile, they have a bit of personality, and they hand her an easy reply. That is the whole job.

## Openers to skip

- **"Hey" or "Hi, how are you?"** It gives her nothing to answer and signals you did not read her profile.
- **Comments only about her looks.** "You're gorgeous" is nice to hear and easy to ignore. Pick something she chose to share.
- **Long paragraphs.** If your opener needs scrolling, it is too much for someone you have not met.
- **Copy and paste lines.** If the same line works on every profile, it works on none of them. Women compare notes.
- **Pickup lines and heavy innuendo.** They might get a laugh from a friend. They rarely get a date from a stranger.
- **Negativity.** Opening with what you dislike about dating apps, her city or her prompt starts the conversation in a bad place.

## Should you use Hinge Convo Starters?

Hinge now offers its own help here. When you tap to send a like in Discover, a feature called [Convo Starters](https://hinge.co/newsroom/convo-starters) can show three tips tied to specific photos or prompts on her profile. It started in the US and has since expanded to the UK. Hinge says the tips do not give you pre-written lines to copy. You still write the message in your own voice, and you can turn the feature off in settings.

Used that way, it is a decent nudge when you are stuck. Hinge says over a third (35%) of daters in early testing felt more confident reaching out with it.

Just do not hand the whole job to AI. This week Match Group CEO Spencer Rascoff made that clear: [according to Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/tinder-and-hinge-ban-ai-agents-from-running-profiles-and-dating-for-you-3427393), he said Match has put guardrails in place to prevent AI agents from being used on Tinder and Hinge, and that the company wants humans reviewing other humans' profiles and sending the messages. ## What to do after she matches back

A great opener earns you a match. It does not earn you a date by itself. Three things to do next:

- **Reply the same day.** Hinge has pushed hard on responsiveness. Its [Your Turn Limits](https://hinge.co/press/your-turn-limits) feature makes people with too many unanswered chats reply or close them before matching again, and Hinge says the test increased responsiveness on the app by 20%. A quick reply keeps you out of the pile.
- **Build on her answer.** If she replied to your taco question, you already have your next topic. Keep it light and specific.
- **Ask for the date early.** After a few good exchanges, suggest a specific plan with two times. My full approach is in [how to ask a girl out on a dating app](https://blog.emerald.dating/how-to-ask-a-girl-out-on-a-dating-app/).

## A five minute daily routine

If you are busy, this is all it takes:

1. **Open Hinge once a day.** Same time, five to ten minutes.
2. **Send fewer, better likes.** Only like profiles you would actually take on a date, and send every one with a comment.
3. **Use the formula.** Detail, a bit of you, easy question. One or two sentences.
4. **Reply to matches in the same sitting.** Move good conversations toward a plan.

Do that for two weeks and compare the number of matches and dates to the two weeks before. That is the only test that counts.

Your profile matters too. A strong opener lands better when your own prompts give her something to reply to. If yours need work, start with [the best Hinge prompts for guys](https://blog.emerald.dating/best-hinge-prompts-for-guys/).

## The bottom line

The best Hinge opening lines are short, specific and easy to answer. Comment on a prompt when you can, show a little of yourself, and end with a question she can reply to in one line.
