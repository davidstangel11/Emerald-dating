You match, the first few messages go well, and then nothing. No reply, no unmatch, just silence. If it happens once, you shrug. If it happens every week, you start wondering what you are doing wrong.

Getting ghosted on dating apps is not a verdict on you. It is mostly a timing and momentum problem, and that means a lot of it is fixable. Here is what the data says and the eight changes I have men make to stop losing good matches in the chat.

## How common ghosting really is

Ghosting is a normal part of app dating now, and it hits everyone. A Tawkify survey of active app users, [reported by Global Dating Insights](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/tawkify-survey-reveals-hidden-time-costs-for-singles/), found people get ghosted an average of four times a year. The same survey found nearly half of matches never turn into an in-person meeting, and a third of users take a week or longer to arrange a first date.

For men, the silence starts even earlier. In Pew Research Center's survey of online daters, [57% of men said they did not receive enough messages](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2020/02/06/users-of-online-dating-platforms-experience-both-positive-and-negative-aspects-of-courtship-on-the-web/), compared with 24% of women. So when a man finally gets a conversation going, losing it hurts more.

It is also in the news again this week. [Global Dating Insights reports](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/from-the-web/friendship-apps-ghosting-bumble-timeleft-222-sonder/) that ghosting has followed people onto friendship apps too. Nobody has solved it. But you can make yourself much harder to ghost.

## Why matches go quiet

Most ghosting is not personal. The common causes are boring:

- **Too many open chats.** She has a dozen conversations going. The ones that feel like work drop off first.
- **No momentum.** The chat drifts into pen pal mode with no plan, so it fades.
- **Slow replies.** A two day gap tells her you are not that interested, so she stops investing.
- **Low effort messages.** "Hey" or "how's your week" gives her nothing to answer.
- **Life.** She met someone, got busy at work or deleted the app.

Hinge has said as much. When it tested its Your Turn Limits feature, it said [44% of daters cite lack of responsiveness as a top dating challenge](https://hinge.co/newsroom/your-turn-limits-test). The feature nudges anyone with eight or more people waiting on a reply to either answer or end those chats before they can keep sending likes.

The most useful number on that page is this one: Hinge's 2023 data showed matches where the first message got a reply within 24 hours were **72% more likely to lead to a date**. Speed is not a small detail. It is the whole game.

## 8 fixes to stop getting ghosted

### 1. Reply within 24 hours, every time

Given Hinge's data, this is the first rule. You do not need to answer in five minutes. You need to answer the same day. Block two short windows, say at lunch and after dinner, and clear every open chat. A busy man who replies daily beats a charming man who replies on Sunday.

### 2. Open with something she can actually answer

A generic opener invites a generic non-reply. Comment on one specific thing in her profile and end with an easy question. "You picked the hardest hike in Colorado as your favorite. Was that a good day or a suffering day?" works far better than "Hey, how's it going?" If you are on Bumble, the [new rules that let men message first](https://blog.emerald.dating/bumble-men-message-first/) make a strong opener even more important.

### 3. Give her something to reply to

Your profile is half the conversation. If your prompts are bland, she has nothing to ask you about and the chat dies after two lines. Use prompts with a specific story or opinion she can react to. Our guide to the [best Hinge prompts for guys](https://blog.emerald.dating/best-hinge-prompts-for-guys/) has examples that start conversations instead of ending them.

### 4. Keep messages short and end with a hook

Long paragraphs feel like homework. Two or three lines is plenty. End most messages with a question or a playful statement that invites a response. If your last message is "Haha nice," you just handed her the exit.

### 5. Ask her out within 5 to 10 messages

This is the fix most men skip. The longer a chat runs without a plan, the more likely it fades. Once there is a bit of back and forth, suggest a specific plan: "I'm enjoying this. Want to grab a drink Thursday at 7? I know a good wine bar near Union Square." Specific day, time and place. That is easy to say yes to, and it filters out people who were never going to meet.

### 6. Move to a plan, not just a phone number

Getting her number feels like progress, but it often just moves the ghosting to text. Lock in the date first, then swap numbers to confirm details. The date is the goal. The number is logistics.

### 7. Confirm the day before, then go quiet

A short confirmation the day before cuts last minute cancellations. "Still on for tomorrow at 7? Looking forward to it." Then stop. You do not need to text all day before a first date. Save the good conversation for the table.

### 8. Send one follow up, then let it go

If she goes quiet, send one light message two or three days later. Something new, not "?" or "Did I say something wrong?" For example: "Just tried that taco place you mentioned. You were right." If she does not reply, unmatch and move on. Chasing a silent match costs you time and confidence, and it rarely works.

## What not to do after you get ghosted

A few habits make things worse:

- **Do not send a guilt message.** "Wow, rude" never brings anyone back, and screenshots travel.
- **Do not double and triple text.** One follow up is fine. Three in a row reads as pressure.
- **Do not take it as a referendum on you.** She may have met someone last weekend. Look for patterns across many chats, not one.
- **Do not ghost others yourself.** If you are not interested, a quick "I enjoyed chatting, but I don't think we're a match. Good luck" takes ten seconds. It keeps your own inbox clean too, which matters on Hinge once you hit eight unanswered chats.

## How to tell if it's you or just the apps

If you get ghosted now and then, that is normal. If most of your conversations die before a date, there is usually a pattern. Check three things:

1. **Where do chats die?** After your opener means your openers are weak. After a few messages means you are not asking her out soon enough. After you ask her out means the ask is vague or the profile did not build enough interest.
2. **How fast are you replying?** Be honest. If your average gap is more than a day, start there.
3. **How many chats are you running?** If you have twenty open conversations, you cannot give any of them real attention. Fewer, better chats book more dates.

Fix one thing at a time for two weeks and watch what changes. This kind of small testing is how men cut their app time in half and still get more dates.
