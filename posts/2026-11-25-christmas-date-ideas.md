December is the best month of the year to ask someone out, and most men waste it. They default to "drinks sometime?", watch her calendar fill with parties and travel, and end up seeing her once before New Year's.

The fix is a specific, festive plan that is easy to say yes to. Below are 12 Christmas date ideas built for first and early dates, plus how to ask and how to fit them into a crowded month.

## Why Christmas dates work so well

People are more open to meeting someone this time of year. In a Bumble survey of 1,000 Americans reported in December 2024, [48% said the holidays are the ideal time to meet a partner](https://www.audacy.com/kearth101/news/single-48-think-the-holidays-are-ideal-to-meet-a-partner), and 39% said they enjoy dating in winter more than any other season.

There is a second reason. A festive plan does half the work for you. Lights, music and a little novelty give you something to talk about, which takes pressure off the first 20 minutes. Hinge's Love and Connection Expert Moe Ari Brown makes a similar point in Hinge's [holiday traditions guide](https://hinge.co/newsroom/social-energy-study-holidays): novel shared experiences make people far less likely to fear rejection. That is exactly the feeling you want on a first date.

The catch is timing. Her December is busy. Yours probably is too. So the plan has to be short, specific and booked early.

## 12 Christmas date ideas, from first date to fifth

### Easy first dates (60 to 90 minutes)

**1. Holiday lights walk plus hot chocolate.** Pick a neighborhood or park known for lights, meet at a cafe nearby, grab something warm and walk. It is cheap, festive and easy to extend if it is going well. Bumble lists both [light trails and hot cocoa](https://bumble.com/date/seasonal/christmas-date-ideas) among its Christmas date ideas, and it is the one I would default to.

**2. A Christmas market.** Most big cities run one through December. You get food, a reason to wander and dozens of small things to comment on. Go on a weeknight to skip the crowds.

**3. A cozy bar with a fireplace or a seasonal menu.** If she prefers a classic drink date, make it a better one. Find the bar in your city that goes all in on holiday decor or seasonal cocktails, and make sure it has good non alcoholic options too. One drink, maybe two.

**4. Ice skating.** Slightly silly, a little physical and full of easy laughs. If neither of you can skate well, even better. Keep it to an hour and finish with something warm nearby.

### Second and third dates (two to three hours)

**5. A holiday movie screening.** Many theaters and bars run classic Christmas movie nights in December. A movie is a weak first date because you cannot talk, but on a second date it is fun, especially with dinner or a drink after.

**6. A class: gingerbread, wreath making or cocktails.** Hands on classes are great early dates because you are side by side, working on something, with natural chances to tease each other. Book a small class so it does not feel like a lecture.

**7. Christmas karaoke or a holiday trivia night.** Low stakes, social and memorable. If she has a big personality, this is a strong pick. If you are not sure, trivia is the safer bet.

**8. A holiday show.** A comedy show, a local Nutcracker, a candlelight concert. Buy tickets early because good December seats go fast, and plan a drink before so you get real conversation time.

### Once things are going well (dates four and up)

**9. A cooking night.** Pick one seasonal dish to make together at your place. It is intimate without being too much, and it shows her how you live. Only suggest this once you have met a few times and the vibe is clearly there.

**10. A day trip to a holiday town.** Many regions have a small town that goes all out with lights, shops and a main street that looks like a movie set. A half day trip feels like a real adventure without the commitment of a weekend away.

**11. A holiday party together.** Bringing her to a friend's party is a meaningful step. Do it once you are clearly dating, give her a quick rundown of who will be there, and do not leave her alone in a corner. I covered when to bring someone into your holiday world in my guide to [dating during the holidays](https://blog.emerald.dating/dating-during-the-holidays/).

**12. Game night or a movie night in.** Hinge's Social Energy Study found that [54% of Gen Z and 43% of millennials globally](https://hinge.co/newsroom/social-energy-study-holidays) often hang out with friends without a specific plan. Relaxed nights in are how a lot of people already spend their time. After a few dates, a board game and a classic holiday movie can feel more connecting than another dinner out.

## How to ask for a Christmas date

The ask matters as much as the idea. Vague asks get vague answers, especially when her calendar is full. Use a simple formula: a hook from your conversation, a specific plan and two time options.

For example:

"You said you have never been to the Christmas market downtown. Let's fix that. Hot chocolate and a walk around, Tuesday or Thursday after work?"

That message works because it is personal, it is clearly a date and it gives her an easy choice instead of an open question. For more on timing and wording, read my guide on [how to ask a girl out on a dating app](https://blog.emerald.dating/how-to-ask-a-girl-out-on-a-dating-app/).

A few rules for December specifically:

- **Ask early in the week.** Weekends fill first. A Tuesday or Wednesday date is easier to get and often less crowded.
- **Book what needs booking.** Classes, shows and popular bars sell out. If you suggest it, have the reservation ready.
- **Have a backup.** If she says she is slammed, offer a 45 minute version: coffee and a lights walk near her office.
- **Do not wait until after the holidays.** "Let's grab drinks in January" usually means the conversation dies over Christmas.

## What to avoid on a Christmas date

Some festive ideas sound fun but work against you early on.

- **Expensive gifts.** On a first or second date, no gift is needed. If you have been seeing each other for a few weeks, keep it small and thoughtful.
- **Big crowds with no plan.** The biggest tree lighting in town on a Saturday night means waiting in lines and shouting to be heard. Go on a weeknight or pick a smaller spot.
- **Anything too long.** A five hour first date plan puts pressure on both of you. Keep it short and leave her wanting more.
- **Family events too soon.** Inviting someone you met two weeks ago to a family dinner is a lot. Save that for later.
- **Ignoring her preferences.** Not everyone celebrates Christmas. If she mentions she does not, choose a winter plan instead: skating, a market or a cozy bar work for almost anyone.

## A simple December game plan

If you are a busy man, here is how I would run the month:

1. **Week one:** Get two or three conversations to a specific ask. Suggest weeknight dates for the second week of December.
2. **Week two:** Go on first dates. Use the easy 60 to 90 minute ideas above.
3. **Week three:** Book a second date with anyone you liked, before travel starts. Make it a class, a show or a movie night out.
4. **Holiday week:** Keep the conversation going with a quick, warm message or two while you are both traveling. Lock in a date for the first week of January before the year ends.

This keeps your pipeline moving through the slowest stretch of the year for most men, and it puts you first in line when January rolls around.

## The bottom line

The best Christmas date ideas are short, specific and a little festive. Lead with a lights walk, a market or a cozy bar for first dates, move to classes and shows for second dates, and save cooking nights and holiday parties for when things are clearly going well. Ask early in the week, book ahead and do not let December slip by.
